I am Hina Suzuki, an undergraduate astronomy major with a minor in electrical engineering. I am passionate about understanding the universe through both theoretical and observational approaches. My research includes black hole simulations, general relativistic radiative transfer, and the development of innovative space-based radio telescope instruments.

I lead the mechanical and electrical design of an inflatable aperture telescope, and I have conducted instrumentation work at the National Radio Astronomy Observatory, including antenna repair, circuit design, and software development for telescope control systems. My computational work focuses on simulating black hole environments and studying photon rings, including those around Kerr black holes and naked singularities.

I aim to pursue a PhD in astronomy, combining theory, computation, and instrumentation to advance our understanding of black holes and their astrophysical environments. This website showcases my research, projects, and academic journey, highlighting my efforts to bridge the gap between theoretical insights and practical exploration of the universe.
